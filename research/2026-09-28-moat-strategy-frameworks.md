# Cornerwork: a defensible business beyond the demo

Date: 28 September 2026
Methods: strategic-consulting-frameworks + market-research, imported from the user's Claude Desktop skill packages into Codex. Strategic lenses: Porter's Five Forces, VRIO, and Blue Ocean's eliminate/reduce/raise/create decisions.

## Decision in plain English

**Build the system that helps an established coaching programme work across its gyms, then earn the right to distribute through that programme.** This is a stronger moat hypothesis than storing conversations, because a competitor would need to replace an adopted operation and win its customer relationships. It is not a proven moat today: Cornerwork has a demonstration, and neither partner demand nor repeat paid usage has been established. Current competitors already offer substantial coaching, check-in and member-context capabilities. The recommendation is a bounded test of a business advantage, not a reason to build a much larger app.

An example: a coach educator runs a six-week beginner programme used by 20 independent gyms. Cornerwork helps each coach know what a member struggled with, what advice they received, and whether someone followed up. The educator introduces Cornerwork to its gyms because it helps deliver the programme consistently. Over time, Cornerwork accumulates paying deployments, programme-specific operating knowledge, and useful feedback about what worked.

The hypothetical 20 gyms are an illustration, not a discovered customer network.

## What was tested

Situation: members attend a class, send feedback or a voice note, receive a coach reply, and get more informed coaching next time. The current demo expresses that loop.

Complication: gym software providers already have distribution, attendance data and established purchasing relationships. A memory feature can be copied. Data stored before a competitor launches is useful only if it continues to improve a result customers value.

Question: what can Cornerwork accumulate that makes winning its customers slower or more expensive than copying its screens?

Initial hypothesis: partner distribution plus an adopted coaching workflow is more defensible than a standalone gym's message history. Research supports testing this, but also weakens any claim that partnerships or platform neutrality are themselves unique: incumbents already pursue both.

```text
Can Cornerwork build an enduring advantage?
|
+-- Customer value: Does it solve a recurring paid problem?
|   +-- Members receive useful follow-up
|   +-- Coaches save time or coach more effectively
|   +-- Owners see enough benefit to renew
|
+-- Competitive protection: What must a clone reproduce?
|   +-- Acquired customer/channel relationships
|   +-- Adopted operating workflow and content rights
|   +-- Evidence that accumulated experience improves delivery
|
+-- Capture and execution: Can Cornerwork keep the benefit?
    +-- Economics after support and partner compensation
    +-- Reliable, permitted access to required platform events
    +-- Data/content permissions and manageable delivery effort
```

Priority: test customer value and partner access first. A technically defensible asset without willing customers has little commercial value.

### Acquisition is plausible, but does not require an uncopyable invention

ABC Fitness announced its acquisition of FitMetrics on 3 September 2026. FitMetrics centralises coaching information and assists with check-ins, analysis and programme updates, with coaches retaining control. Its founder developed it from operating a coaching business using Trainerize. This is a particularly relevant precedent for a focused product that complements an incumbent. The announcement does not reveal the purchase price or establish which asset determined the buyer's decision. Its capacity-improvement claims are company-reported, not evidence Cornerwork can deliver the same results. [ABC announcement](https://abcfitness.com/press-release/ai-agents-fitmetrics/)

ABC also acquired Replify in July 2026 for conversational AI capabilities across gym sales and service. The announcement describes deployments with shared customers before acquisition. That supports a plausible path from useful complementary product to strategic purchase; it does not establish a general acquisition formula. [Replify announcement](https://abcfitness.com/press-release/abc-fitness-acquires-replify-agentic-ai/)

**Practical conclusion:** a buyer can rationally purchase a copyable product for speed, customers, an experienced team and a proven operation. Do not optimise Cornerwork around making Gymdesk unable to build. Optimise around proving a valuable business that a buyer could accelerate.

## Buyer and adoption research hypotheses

The initial segment should be adult beginner programmes with repeated classes, multiple coaches and a clear programme owner. Combat sports provides initial context; beginner strength, Pilates or other coached activities are expansion candidates. Sport is secondary to the repeated handoff problem.

| Role | Proposed need / buying trigger | Barrier | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gym owner, economic buyer | A beginner programme is growing; follow-up becomes inconsistent | Another bill, uncertain return, staff adoption | Paid pilot and renewal without member upsell being required |
| Coach educator / small network operator, possible channel or buyer | Wants its programme delivered consistently across independent gyms | Existing tools, reluctance to endorse a startup, support burden | Introductions to real sites and a programme owner willing to participate |
| Coach, daily user | Wants the relevant context before class without searching messages | Extra admin and irrelevant notifications | Voluntary pre-class use and completed follow-ups |
| Adult member, participant | Wants timely help and continuity between coaches | App fatigue, privacy concerns, low motivation to report | Repeat voluntary participation and useful responses |

These are interview hypotheses, not findings from conducted customer interviews. Age, income and gender are not established segmentation variables here; inventing personas would add false precision. Buyer behaviour and workflow matter more initially.

Charging the gym does not remove member adoption from the business model. Even if the gym pays reliably at first, it may cancel when coaches or members stop using the product. At $10 per member, 5–10 members cover a $50–100 software bill in gross revenue, before coach labour, payment costs or other expenses. Member resale is an optional packaging test, not proof of retention or profit.

## Framework 1: Five Forces

Qualitative assessments below are analyst judgements grounded in the product evidence, not industry scores.

```text
Force                 Pressure on Cornerwork   Strategic response
--------------------  -----------------------  ------------------------------
Existing rivalry      High                     Own one specific programme job
New entrants          High for core features   Accumulate adoption and access
Substitutes           High                     Demonstrate less work / value
Buyer bargaining      High initially           Tie renewal to visible benefit
Supplier bargaining   Material                 Verify access; avoid one gatekeeper
```

The unattractive position is an optional AI memory add-on that an incumbent bundles for free. A more promising position is the delivery system for a programme the buyer already cares about, with an organisation helping secure adoption. Supplier power remains: two integrations reduce concentration but add maintenance, and do not eliminate contractual dependency. No current API access entitlement or production integration is assumed.

## Framework 2: VRIO — what would actually qualify as a moat?

V = valuable; R = rare; I = costly to imitate; O = organised to capture the benefit. The last column is deliberately stricter than 'useful feature'.

```text
Resource                        V       R       I       O       Verdict
------------------------------  ------  ------  ------  ------  -----------------
Pings, voice notes, summaries    Maybe   No      No      Demo    Product features
Stored member conversations     Maybe   Local*  Low     Demo    Not a moat alone
Two provider integrations       Yes**   No      Modest  No      Enabling capability
Adopted partner programme       Maybe   Maybe   Maybe   No      Strongest test
Permissioned outcome history    Maybe   Maybe   Maybe   No      Later, conditional
Recognised member passport      Maybe   No***   High*** No      Too early
```

*The exact records are unique, but their commercial usefulness may be limited and customers should be able to export their information.
**Assuming customers need them and access is permitted.
***A profile screen is easy to copy; widespread recognition and trusted contributors could be hard. Neither exists here.

Stored inputs and outputs become more defensible only when they produce a better decision: the next coach knows which intervention was tried, whether it helped, and what remains unresolved. Even then, a large incumbent may build a similar dataset faster. The advantage must show up in measurable delivery quality or lower operating cost, not record count.

Do not call this a network effect until adding one participating organisation makes the product more useful to others. Shared infrastructure, referrals and accumulating usage are not automatically network effects.

## Borrowed mechanisms from other domains

### 1. Xero: win an intermediary who brings customers

Xero provides workflows for accounting partners to manage clients. The lesson is to make the intermediary's work better, rather than merely pay for referrals. For Cornerwork, that intermediary could be an independent coach educator or programme operator with a reachable group of gyms. [Xero partner programme](https://www.xero.com/us/partner-programme/)

What to build first: a repeatable setup for one programme, clear coach responsibilities and a useful follow-up record. What to secure: actual introductions, an agreed rollout owner, and commercial terms that leave support affordable.

Counterevidence: ABC already partners with IPTA to reach trainers through education. 'Partner with certification organisations' is not an undiscovered strategy. Cornerwork needs a specific underserved partner and an operating benefit the partner prefers. [ABC/IPTA announcement](https://abcfitness.com/press-release/abc-fitness-ipta-partnership/)

### 2. CrossFit / Les Mills: distribute a teaching system, not empty software

CrossFit supplies affiliate programming and coaching material. Les Mills Connect connects instructor qualifications and club operations. These illustrate how content, standards and participating organisations can reinforce adoption. They do not prove a new entrant can reproduce those networks. [CrossFit programming](https://www.crossfit.com/affiliate-toolkit/crossfit-affiliate-programming), [Les Mills Connect](https://www.lesmills.com/uk/instructors/learn-develop/les-mills-connect)

Cornerwork's version: license a partner's programme where appropriate, then help coaches execute its follow-ups. Any preferred-provider or exclusive-content agreement must be genuinely negotiated; assume no exclusivity today. Avoid inventing a certification nobody recognises.

### 3. CARFAX: the useful asset is trusted history with contributors

CARFAX describes a large network of information sources. The transferable mechanism is provenance: knowing who supplied a record and what happened. A member passport alone does not reproduce that advantage. [CARFAX data sources](https://support.carfax.com/article/where-does-carfax-get-its-information2)

Cornerwork's modest first version: record who gave advice, which class or programme it related to, and the subsequent member/coach assessment. Do not label subjective reports as objective proof of skill, recovery or causation. Defer cross-gym portability until customers show real demand.

## Framework 3: eliminate / reduce / raise / create

```text
ELIMINATE from this moat experiment  REDUCE
- Billing replacement               - Repeated data entry
- Public marketplace                - Unnecessary member prompts
- Universal certification           - Coach search through old threads
- Medical/return-to-training claims  - Bespoke setup for every gym

RAISE                               CREATE
- Continuity between coaches        - One partner's repeatable programme setup
- Clear ownership of follow-ups     - Advice -> follow-up -> reported result
- Relevant pre-class information    - A channel with demonstrated paid rollout
```

This is a proposed position to test, not a claim of an uncontested market. It narrows the job to keeping a programme's coaching promises across multiple instructors. It can extend outside combat sports because the reusable unit is a programme, class, member and responsible coach; sport-specific content stays configurable.

## What the product must remember

Example, not a validated user story:

```text
Programme: beginner boxing, week 2
Member: says they lose balance after a combination
Coach A: assigns one specific drill
Next class: Coach B sees the drill and checks the issue
Follow-up: member reports improvement / no change / did not try
Record: source, timing and assessment remain attached
```

Existing input/output storage covers the first half. The extra requirement is a completed follow-up tied to the advice, with enough context to guide the next coach. Do not collect more fields unless they change a decision. A transcript warehouse, an unanswered message queue and a completed coaching loop have different business value.

The strongest eventual claim would be: 'Our partner gyms consistently complete these follow-ups with less staff effort, and the programme operator brings us additional locations.' Neither half has been demonstrated yet.

## Economics and the buy-versus-build argument

Illustrative monthly unit economics, not measured costs:

| Item | $75 monthly location |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | $75 |
| Infrastructure/messages, assumed | -$15 |
| Partner commission, assumed 20% | -$15 |
| Remaining before support and fixed costs | $45 |
| 30 minutes support at assumed $60/hour | -$30 |
| Remaining before fixed costs | $15 |

At this price, a partnership that creates lots of bespoke service can destroy the benefit of cheap distribution. Measure setup hours and ongoing support. A network package or programme licence may eventually support higher revenue, but only customer evidence can justify it.

A buyer would have a more concrete reason to buy if Cornerwork can show:

1. Paying locations and repeatable partner-led acquisition the buyer does not already have.
2. Renewal and observed coach usage, with credible operating-cost or customer-benefit evidence.
3. Deployments, integration knowledge and an experienced team that accelerate rollout.
4. Clear rights to the relevant technology and content; appropriately permissioned customer data.
5. Commercial agreements and customer relationships that survive a transaction where required.

Acquisition remains optional. A strategically useful business may never receive an offer. Exclusivity, channel rights and customer commitments cannot be assumed transferable. Neutrality across competing booking platforms could also become less credible after acquisition; that trade-off belongs in the strategy.

## Risks and falsification

Likelihood is an informed judgement for this proposed model, not a statistical estimate.

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Response / disconfirming evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incumbent bundles the core feature | High | High | Customers must prefer a specific programme operation or channel benefit, not just summaries |
| Partner already has an adequate tool | High | Medium-high | Review its actual workflow before building; stop if no meaningful gap |
| Coaches ignore the brief | High | Medium-high | Observe real pre-class use; do not count founder reminders as adoption |
| Partner introductions do not convert | High | Unknown | Require actual paid deployments before calling it distribution |
| API access or event quality fails | High | Unknown | Verify provider approval, actual attendance events and failure handling before promising automation |
| Support consumes margin | High | Medium | Track hours and actual communication costs per location |
| History does not improve decisions | Medium-high | Unknown | Compare use and decisions with/without history; do not build a model simply because data exists |
| Sensitive information discourages use | High | Unknown | Minimise collection, explain access and retention, and verify applicable obligations before production |
| Owners cut optional subscriptions | Medium-high | Unknown | Test renewal and measurable value; avoid assuming general industry growth protects spend |

No legal conclusion or specific compliance regime is asserted here. This research did not establish a regulatory barrier that would protect Cornerwork.

## A bounded 90-day plan

Assumption: approximately 10 founder hours per week, to be reconfirmed. This is a learning plan, not a promise to complete two production integrations within that budget.

| Period | Owner / work | Deliverable | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Founder: interview 4 reachable educators/programme owners and 8 coaches/owners; inspect existing tools | One documented recurring handoff failure, current workaround and buying responsibility | Continue only if a real programme owner wants to test it |
| Weeks 3–4 | Founder + willing partner: define one programme and recruit 3 paying locations | Named rollout owner, agreed price, baseline staff effort and written pilot scope | If introductions cannot produce paid interest, partner distribution remains unproven |
| Weeks 5–8 | Founder: run the smallest workable delivery flow; verify production prerequisites separately | Usage, follow-up completion, support cost and member responses | Fix the operational bottleneck before adding functionality |
| Weeks 9–12 | Founder + sites: assess renewal and another introduction; interview one adjacent activity | Paid renewal decision and documented expansion reason | Expand only after evidence of use, value and affordable support |

Interview prompts: 'Show me the last time another coach needed a member's context.' 'What happened when follow-up was missed?' 'What tool did you use?' 'Who would approve payment?' 'Can your current software already solve this?' Ask for concrete incidents before presenting the solution.

Proposed pilot gates, to agree before launch; these are experimental thresholds, not market benchmarks:

- At least 2 of 3 gyms renew at the agreed paid price.
- Coaches voluntarily consult the brief before at least 60% of eligible observed classes during the final two weeks.
- At least 70% of follow-ups due in the measurement period are completed by their agreed due class/date. Exclude cases with no due follow-up; report counts alongside percentages.
- Measure total staff minutes, not just app time. A faster interface with more work is not a win.
- Partner produces at least 2 additional qualified introductions with an identified buyer. An introduction is not a sale.
- Ongoing support and actual variable costs leave positive contribution after partner compensation; do not hide unpaid founder labour.

Three sites cannot establish a causal retention lift. Track retention as exploratory context; use paid renewal and observed behaviour for the immediate decision. If the programme route fails but direct gyms retain strongly, keep the useful product and reassess distribution rather than manufacturing a partner narrative.
